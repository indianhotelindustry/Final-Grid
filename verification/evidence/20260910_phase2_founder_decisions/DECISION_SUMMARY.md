# The Seven Decisions — one-page Founder summary

Prepared 2026-09-10 from the repository at `a84566ae`. Plain language; the technical evidence is in `FOUNDER_DECISION_PACK.md`.

| Decision | In simple words | My recommendation | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FD-P2-01 Who runs the hotel (MP-D9)** | The system has five job roles built in, but nobody has said which ones the hotel will actually use, or whether the owner can approve their own actions when there is no second person. Today there is exactly one login, the Admin. | Keep the current "Admin/Manager" working assumption and write down its limit: it stays valid only while no Front-Desk, Accountant or Housekeeping login exists; the Admin may approve their own request only when nobody else can, with a written reason. If you can simply tell us the hotel is a one-operator property, rule that instead. | Without this, the approval-workflow phase (2b) cannot define who approves whom, and the moment a second staff login is created the financial reports are visible to that person. |
| **FD-P2-02 Stop deleting the audit trail** | A background job deletes every audit record older than 90 days. From about 7 November it would start erasing the record of who did what to the money. Storage is not a reason: a year of records is a few megabytes. | Approve a small, separate fix that stops deleting audit records (other technical logs can still be pruned). Keep it off until a proper archiving design is approved. | The audit trail is the proof behind every payment and correction. Deleting it silently would undo the protection Phase 1 added. |
| **FD-P2-03 The eight test rows (D11)** | Eight old payments and charges from commissioning have no folio. You already ruled they stay exactly as they are. The verification report therefore says FAIL, permanently, and a certification cannot be signed on a FAIL. | Certify with a **declared exception**: the certification lists those eight rows by number and amount and states that the FAIL consists of them and nothing else. Any ninth row would be a real failure. Nothing is changed in the data or the rules. | It lets certification be honest and signable without touching history or weakening the rule. |
| **FD-P2-04 What "verified backup" means (PD-006)** | Having a backup file is not the same as knowing it can bring the hotel back. We need an agreed checklist that says a restore is verified. | Adopt a twelve-point checklist: the backup's fingerprint matches, it restores cleanly, the database is intact, the structure and every table's contents match the original, the verification engine gives the same answers, the encrypted backup has been restored on a *different* machine with the key held under a written custody rule, and all of it is recorded with who did it and when. | Today the hotel's own automatic backups have never been restored anywhere. Recovery is a mandatory gate before going live. |
| **FD-P2-05 How the nightly close runs** | The night audit posts the room charges for the day and moves the business date forward. It can run itself at 2 am, or a person can run it at the end of the day. On the live system it is currently set to run by a person. | Keep it **run by a person** for the first release, with a written daily-close routine. Automate later, after the rules for unattended financial jobs are written. | An unattended job that moves money and dates with nobody watching is exactly what your existing ruling (FD-009) forbids until it is properly controlled. |
| **FD-P2-06 Two rule mismatches (INV-B06, INV-D02)** | Two verification rules fire on normal business: taking a deposit before a guest arrives, and refunding a deposit when a booking is cancelled. The business behaviour is right; the rules were written without those cases. | Keep the business behaviour. Refine the two rules so that deposits before arrival and cancellation refunds are recognised as legitimate, while still catching genuinely wrong dates or untraceable reversals. No money or tax effect. | Otherwise every real deposit and refund would show up as a "violation" and the verification report would become noise. |
| **FD-P2-07 Who must approve what (maker-checker)** | Some actions should need two people: one to request, one to approve. Others just need the right role. We need the list before Phase 2b can start. | Adopt a four-tier list: two-person approval for voids/refunds, corrections on closed days, large folio transfers, reopening a closed day, large forfeits and credits; single authorized role for everyday postings; alerts only for the rest; and an Admin emergency override that always requires a written reason and is reviewed at the next close. You choose one rupee threshold for "large transfer" and the self-approval rule. | This is the entry ticket for Phase 2b and the control that makes the audit trail meaningful. |
